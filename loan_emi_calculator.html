What is an EMI Calculator?

An EMI (Equated Monthly Installment) calculator is a financial tool that helps borrowers estimate the fixed monthly payment required to repay a loan over a specified period. EMIs consist of two components: the principal repayment and the interest charged on the outstanding loan balance.
The EMI formula is: EMI = P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ ÷ ((1 + r)ⁿ − 1), where P is the principal loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n is the number of monthly installments. This formula ensures that each installment remains constant throughout the loan tenure.
Understanding your EMI before taking a loan is crucial for financial planning. It allows you to assess whether the monthly repayment fits within your budget, compare different loan offers, and understand the total cost of borrowing including interest. Banks and financial institutions in Bangladesh and across the world use this formula for all standard loan products.
The three key factors that determine your EMI are: the principal amount (the higher the loan, the higher the EMI), the interest rate (higher rates increase the EMI significantly), and the loan tenure (longer tenures reduce the monthly EMI but increase the total interest paid over time).
Home loans, car loans, personal loans, and education loans all use the EMI model. For example, a home loan of BDT 30,00,000 at an annual rate of 9% for 20 years would result in a monthly EMI of approximately BDT 27,000, with total interest exceeding the original loan amount over the full tenure.
Using an EMI calculator before committing to a loan empowers you to make smarter financial decisions. You can experiment with different loan amounts, interest rates, and tenures to find a combination that balances affordable monthly payments with minimizing total interest costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does EMI stand for?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Installment. It is a fixed payment amount made by a borrower to a lender at a specified date each month. EMIs are used to pay off both interest and principal every month so that the loan is fully paid off over the loan tenure.

Does a longer tenure mean lower EMI?

Yes. A longer repayment tenure reduces your monthly EMI, making it more affordable. However, you end up paying significantly more in total interest over the life of the loan. It is generally advisable to choose the shortest tenure you can comfortably afford.

How does the interest rate affect my EMI?

Even a small change in the interest rate has a significant impact on your EMI and total repayment. For example, a 1% increase on a large loan can add tens of thousands in total interest. Always compare rates across lenders before committing.

What is the difference between flat rate and reducing balance rate?

A flat interest rate is calculated on the full principal for the entire tenure. A reducing balance rate is calculated only on the outstanding principal after each EMI payment, which is more favorable to borrowers. This calculator uses the reducing balance method, which is the most common.

Can I use this for home loans, car loans, and personal loans?

Yes. The EMI formula is the same for all loan types that use a reducing balance method. Simply enter the loan amount, annual interest rate, and tenure in months, and you will get the correct monthly installment for any loan type.

Is my financial data safe?

Absolutely. All calculations happen locally within your browser. No data is transmitted to any server, logged, or stored in any way. You can use this calculator with complete privacy.
