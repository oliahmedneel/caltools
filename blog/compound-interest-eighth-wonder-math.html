Compound Interest: The Eighth Wonder of the World and Its Math

E
Elena Rostov Published on Mar 28, 2026
🌱

Albert Einstein famously called compound interest "the eighth wonder of the world. He who understands it, earns it; he who doesn't, pays it." While it sounds like complex financial jargon, the mathematics behind compounding is actually a simple percentage formula with massive wealth-building power.

In this article, we'll explain how compound interest works, explore the Rule of 72, and show how starting early gives you an exponential advantage.

1. Simple vs. Compound Interest

To understand the magic of compounding, you must first distinguish it from simple interest:

  • Simple Interest: You earn interest only on your initial deposit. If you invest $1,000 at a 10% simple annual rate, you earn $100 every single year. After 10 years, you have $2,000.
  • Compound Interest: You earn interest on your initial deposit plus any accumulated interest. In Year 1, your $1,000 earns $100, bringing your balance to $1,100. In Year 2, your 10% interest is calculated on $1,100, earning you $110. After 10 years, you have $2,593. Compounding gave you an extra $593 for free!

2. The Rule of 72: A Mental Math Shortcut

Want to know how long it will take for your investment to double in value? Use the Rule of 72, a brilliant mental math shortcut. Simply divide 72 by your annual interest rate:

  • At 6% interest rate: 72 / 6 = 12 years to double your money.
  • At 8% interest rate: 72 / 8 = 9 years to double your money.
  • At 12% interest rate: 72 / 12 = 6 years to double your money.

3. The Exponential Power of Time

Because compounding interest scales exponentially, time is your greatest asset. An investor who starts saving $100 a month at age 20 will accumulate significantly more wealth by age 60 than an investor who starts saving $300 a month at age 35, even though the second investor puts in far more of their own cash!
